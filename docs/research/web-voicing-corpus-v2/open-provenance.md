# Structured open sources: provenance and acquisition audit

Access date: 2026-09-13. This arm independently searched guitar chord databases, JSON fingering libraries, chord applications, author contributions, and generator projects. Search terms included `github guitar chord database curated chord fingerings license`, `github guitar chord voicings dataset generated manually`, `guitar chord database manually`, `2633 guitar chords dataset source`, and `guitar chord data chordpro origin database`. Primary repository READMEs, licenses, file histories, data formats, and upstream credits—not package popularity or diagram appearance—determined classification. Search results repeatedly converged on the same mirrors and calculators. Continued ingestion of those would enlarge row counts without resolving editorial origin.

## Reusable inventory and what it means

| Source | Observed inventory | Source origin classification | Curated-family votes |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| tombatossals/chords-db guitar file | 3,283 voicing rows | UNKNOWN original creation; demonstrable contributor edits | 0 |
| ChordPro standard guitar config | 1,155 records, including 488 aliases and 2 no-chord records | UNKNOWN initial shapes; named contributor and product default selection | 0 |
| UCI Guitar Chords finger positions | 2,632 data rows | UNKNOWN; software checking is stated | 0 |

These are three acquisition sources/provisional lineage buckets, **not three established independent human-curation families**. The two sources with frets provide product-reference diagnostics only. UCI preserves allocations and pitch spellings, but lacks absolute frets and pitch octaves, so exact voicings cannot be recovered confidently. Its advertised 2,633 instances appears to include the header; our CSV parser observes 2,632 data records. No inference converts fingers 1–4 into frets 1–4. The [UCI landing page](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/575/guitar%2Bchords%2Bfinger%2Bpositions) states CC BY 4.0; its [license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) permits reuse with attribution. No donor name or manual-selection method was found on that primary page.

The [chords-db README](https://github.com/tombatossals/chords-db) documents contributions and barre representation, but does not establish how the original inventory was selected. Its actual [MIT license](https://github.com/tombatossals/chords-db/blob/df06fa7b425cf5fd29485ff6591236b3557e3fac/LICENSE) credits David Rubert. Human corrections are visible, e.g. [C major correction](https://github.com/tombatossals/chords-db/commit/8083b340eda7efc1ac9dc668c2170c16d53b1366). Corrections alone cannot certify every original shape as manual. The library is acquired from pinned upstream, not silently accepted from an installed dependency.

The [ChordPro instrument configuration](https://www.chordpro.org/chordpro/chordpro-configuration-instrument/) documents guitar defaults and chord naming. Its [guitar source](https://github.com/ChordPro/chordpro/blob/4b25408d830bd3f4c53fc6fb544b2babe4da09e1/lib/ChordPro/res/config/guitar.rjson) explicitly enumerates entries and aliases. The [March 2026 contribution](https://github.com/ChordPro/chordpro/commit/aced0a2043ed3664475804c980f24eb0d55d7612) names rkj90266; a subsequent commit adds A/E. This supports maintained product selection, while import/manual/generation origin remains unresolved. The [Artistic License 2.0](https://github.com/ChordPro/chordpro/blob/4b25408d830bd3f4c53fc6fb544b2babe4da09e1/LICENSE) is retained with verbatim raw files. Normalized output is explicitly a modified research representation, not a replacement ChordPro distribution. ChordPro and chords-db share 258 exact physical shapes among 3,070 and 474 unique shapes respectively (the latter includes the all-muted no-chord sentinel). This overlap is descriptive; familiar shapes alone prove neither independence nor copying. Do not turn it into 258 independently curated confirmations.

## Demonstrated lineage and contaminated apparent replication

* [chordbook/chords-db](https://github.com/chordbook/chords-db) is explicitly a fork of tombatossals. [optilude/guitar-practice](https://github.com/optilude/guitar-practice) documents vendoring the same guitar JSON; its separate triad collection is generated. [ChordMiniApp](https://github.com/ptnghia-j/ChordMiniApp) names the same dependency. These inherit the `tombatossals-chords-db` family for those records.
* [T-vK/chord-collection](https://github.com/T-vK/chord-collection) credits JGuitar and asserts GPL. [JGuitar](https://jguitar.com/) explicitly describes dynamic chord calculation across instruments and tunings. This is the `jguitar-calculator` family, not an independent human dictionary. [szaza/guitar-chords-db-json](https://github.com/szaza/guitar-chords-db-json) calls itself a filtered T-vK version, credits both T-vK and Tombatossals, and exposes an MIT license. That downstream badge does not resolve the upstream GPL or JGuitar redistribution boundary. Do not acquire its ~99,230 advertised rows as fresh votes.
* [Siege Analytics MuseScore plugin](https://github.com/siege-analytics/musescore4-chord-library-plugin) advertises 820 hand-curated standard-tuning entries, yet the inspected pinned `plugin/data/voicings.json` contains **48 `calculated`-tagged rows, 42 `chords-db`-tagged rows, 408 six-string rows and 412 seven-string rows**. The [importer](https://github.com/siege-analytics/musescore4-chord-library-plugin/blob/ca423faec075495505393e078bf17687fc3be1d8/scripts/import_chords_db.py) explicitly pulls chords-db. It also maps major to maj7, minor to min7 and other qualities in ways that require row-level harmonic auditing; an imported label is not a trustworthy engine formula. Remaining records are not automatically manual just because they lack `calculated` tags. The repository describes extraction from published method books; README says CC BY 4.0 while the LICENSE surfaced as Apache-2.0. Retain source metadata and the above aggregates only; no raw books, lessons, extracted rule corpora or voicing collection are copied here. This source is MIXED / lineage-linked, with independent curated support unestablished.
* [bjesus/chords](https://github.com/bjesus/chords) credits its database to Brage Fuglseth's Fretboard and GPL-3.0. That establishes a shared upstream, not manual origin. Song retrieval from Ultimate Guitar is a separate construct and is not acquired.
* [hyvyys/chord-fingering](https://github.com/hyvyys/chord-fingering), [chordictionaryjs](https://github.com/greird/chordictionaryjs), and [Conchordance](https://github.com/ConchordanceProject/Conchordance) explicitly calculate/generate shapes. [GTDB](https://gtdb.org/) explicitly derives chords for alternative tunings from an algorithm. These are algorithm controls, excluded from human-selection counts regardless of how many outputs they expose.

## Reproduction and normalization safeguards

Run `node scripts/web-voicing-corpus-v2/acquire-open.mjs` from the repository root. It uses only Node built-ins, pinned Git revisions, and the public UCI archive. Cached raw bytes are checked against `open-acquisition-lock.json`; `--refresh` fetches and verifies the same hashes. A changed source hash fails closed. Raw licenses and UCI attribution stay beside source files under `.research/web-voicing-corpus-v2/acquired/`. `open-voicings.json` preserves every row, raw native fields, labels, source order, aliases and source fingering. The lock contains acquisition URLs, revisions, byte sizes and SHA-256 values.

The normalized string order is high E to low E. Source arrays are low E to high E and reversed explicitly. Positive relative frets gain `baseFret - 1` (or ChordPro `base - 1`); muted and open strings remain unchanged. All 3,283 chords-db derived sounding MIDI lists match their source MIDI exactly. Its `capo` boolean controls drawing a barre and must not become an actual capo shift. ChordPro alias chains resolve explicitly and retain the original alias identity. Unsupported labels are preserved for the integrated mapping audit; they are not coerced here. ChordPro's conventional default for a label is recorded as product-default selection, not a claim about author/player preference. UCI has `statesHighToLow: null` and an explicit missing-frets exclusion. No source absence becomes negative evidence.

The extraction makes no claim of human preference validation. No source provided a representative preference, comfort, difficulty or playability study. Until original selection and independent origins are established, these open collections can test external product agreement and normalization; they cannot establish independent canonicality bands or protected generalized idiomaticity.
