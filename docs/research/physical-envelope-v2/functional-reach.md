# Functional reach evidence for Physical Envelope Study v2

Research-only contribution, 2026-09-13. Scope: [scope-check.md](C:/Projects/Guitar/docs/research/physical-envelope-v2/scope-check.md). No production code or verdict was changed. Original studies and author-hosted papers were preferred; abstract-only access is identified below. Searches covered active finger spreads, functional thumb/finger workspaces, piano execution, and guitar-specific anthropometry/execution. This is a targeted primary-source review, not a systematic-review completeness claim.

**Finding:** usable measured reference spans exist, including the index–little pair that omits the thumb. They support an explicitly named *flat-hand span reference*. They do not establish a population distribution of simultaneous guitar contact configurations. Available task studies reinforce the need to separate reach, orientation, load and execution.

## Direct span reference with useful distribution summaries

**FR01 — Boyle, Boyle & Booker (2015), Pianist Hand Spans.** Author-hosted conference paper; methods pp. 5–8, Tables 2/4/20/21, Figure 6. Measurements are maximum unaided spread on a flat surface, outside fingertip edges, right hand; 1–5 denotes thumb–little, 2–5 index–little. Samples were nonrandom: adult pianists, principally Australian, and Melbourne business students. Values below convert the paper's rounded centimetres to millimetres, preserving that precision. [Primary paper](https://paskpiano.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Boyle-Boyle-Booker-_APPCA-2015_Dec-2015.pdf)

Pianist paired-span regressions report R²=.39 male/.56 female; no full joint covariance was extracted. Quartiles describe observed samples, not population coverage guarantees. Unequal student pair counts matter. Piano-derived “small-hand” performance zones are not adopted.

**Interpretation proposed by this review:** use this table as a clearly named reference distribution, not a guitar capacity distribution. Retain the sample and side instead of relabeling it “normal adult hand.” Do not pool the recorded sex strata with arbitrary weights. Do not mix fingertip-edge spans with pad-center distances without a declared landmark transformation; no measured transformation is supplied here. Right-hand references do not become individually measured left fretting-hand capacities because group averages are similar.

The index–little measurement is more relevant to a non-thumb fretting spread than thumb–little span, but it still has free intermediate digits and an unloaded flat posture. Simultaneous fretting may require flexion, contact orientation and clearance that this test does not impose. Conversely, a candidate's longitudinal projection need not consume all the measured planar spread in the same anatomical direction. These are construct differences, not a proposed correction factor.

## Guitar-specific span data: useful but limited

**FR02 — Kamaşak Arpaçay et al. (2026), Evaluation of Hand Anthropometric Measurements in Amateur Guitar Players.** Primary journal PDF, Methods/Table 1/Table 2: 70 guitarists and 70 nonplayers, each 34 female/36 male, medical students aged 18–25 in Kırşehir, Türkiye. All right-hand dominant; left hands measured. Guitar experience: 2–10 years. Span is thumb-tip to little-tip at full abduction; assistance/active standardization is insufficiently specified for equivalence to FR01. Guitarists' median span **201.92 mm**, Q1–Q3 **187.10–217.27**; controls **200.17 mm**, **183.05–208.28**; p=.178. Parentheses are quartiles, not extrema. No span SD, sex-stratified span distribution, pair-span covariance or chord execution labels are provided. [Primary paper](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/5401241)

**Disposition:** contextual reference only. This small single-center cross-sectional sample cannot establish training effects or whether a particular voicing is executable. Reporting cautions include an unrelated patient/hospitalization caption in Table 1 and prose/table disagreement over wrist-diameter significance. Those issues do not demonstrate that the span row is wrong, but justify independent clarification before adopting its distribution as a calibration anchor. Do not import the paper's broader aptitude or necessary-strength conclusions into Physical policy.

## Active range, passive range and task execution are separate constructs

**FR03 — Wagner (2012), Musicians' Hand Problems: Looking at Individuality.** This primary investigator's methodological account distinguishes active ranges from joint resistance measured under specified external torque. Its instrument-focused battery includes active spans for all ten digit pairs, passive spreading, and strength; it explicitly cautions against practical decisions from one percentile value and discusses compensation among dimensions. It reports a professional-pianist middle–ring span range of 42–112 mm, but the underlying cohort/table and full distribution were not recovered here; that range is therefore **not** an envelope parameter. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.christoph-wagner-musikphysiologie.de/Ch.%20Wagner_Musicians'%20Hands%20Problems_MPPA_2012.pdf)

The original 1988 *The pianist's hand: anthropometry and biomechanics*, DOI 10.1080/00140138808966651, was located bibliographically, but publisher full text could not be accessed in this search. No primary-verified numeric 1988 parameter table is imported from secondary summaries. [Bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3359991/)

**Review interpretation:** an active maximum records what a person voluntarily achieves under a particular protocol. A passive range records excursion under external assistance/load and says nothing by itself about voluntary control or sufficient force at that position. Neither supplies an endurance or comfort limit. Combining passive bounds with active task claims would silently enlarge the modeled capability domain. Joint ranges measured one axis at a time also must not be treated as independent coordinates of an achievable simultaneous pose.

**FR04 — Lee (2010), Hand Biomechanics in Skilled Pianists Playing a Scale in Thirds.** Author-uploaded paper, DOI 10.21091/mppa.2010.4034. Twelve skilled pianists, eight female/four male, ages 17–44, studied 8 to over 30 years. Measures included active pair spreading on a flat surface and keyboard reach, plus an aggregate span variable. This aggregate is **not simply a thumb–little endpoint distance** and is not interchangeable with FR01. Following two weeks of practice, MIDI execution was evaluated for tempo, articulation and voicing. Wrist ulnar deviation correlated with tempo (r=.89, p=.01); other measured biomechanical features did not correlate with the stated performance criteria. Small sample and specific technique prohibit generalization to all piano tasks or guitars. No usable population pair-span distribution is imported. [Primary author upload](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/49695079_Hand_Biomechanics_in_Skilled_Pianists_Playing_a_Scale_in_Thirds)

**FR05 — Sakai et al. (2006), Hand Span and Digital Motion on the Keyboard.** Primary abstract/institutional record, DOI 10.1016/j.jhsa.2006.02.009. Ten pianists performed an octave (167-mm key separation) and a chord while thumb/little-finger abduction was tracked. Smaller-span participants used greater thumb abduction in the reported comparisons; little-finger abduction did not differ. Full group cutoffs, sex/age composition and angle distributions were not verified from full text, so no angle bounds are extracted. This is executed, task-specific movement evidence; it is not a maximal-span percentile study or a guitar reach threshold. The suggested injury mechanism is not an independently validated safety bound. [Primary institutional abstract](https://researchoutput.ncku.edu.tw/en/publications/hand-span-and-digital-motion-on-the-keyboard-concerns-of-overuse-/)

## Simultaneous functional workspace evidence

**FR06 — Kuo et al. (2009), Functional Workspace for Precision Manipulation between Thumb and Fingers in Normal Hands.** Primary abstract, DOI 10.1016/j.jelekin.2008.07.008. Twenty participants' maximal fingertip trajectories were video-recorded. Functional workspace was defined by positions where thumb-tip and a fingertip can coincide, calculated from maximal workspaces. Functional/maximal ratios for index/middle/ring/little were 33.7/27.1/23.5/19.1%. These are **workspace ratios**, not fractions of linear reach or probabilities of successful use. Age/sex details, absolute workspace distributions and covariance were not verified in the accessible abstract. The authors describe the work as needing further validation. No guitar parameter is extracted. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18778954/)

**FR07 — Bullock, Feix & Dollar (2014), Dexterous Workspace of Human Two- and Three-Fingered Precision Manipulation.** Primary author-hosted conference paper. Nineteen right-handed participants, six male/13 female, ages 18–31, mostly New Haven graduate students, manipulated a 33–41-mm object while maintaining contacts. Two fingers meant thumb+index; three added middle. Hand lengths: 155–198 mm, median 175. Object-center workspace was measured relative to the hand using tracking and voxel occupancy; mean volumes were **5.7 cm³** and **4.8 cm³**, respectively. Within-person normalization produced approximately 38–40% larger two-finger workspace; this is not the ratio of the two grand means. Absolute volume depends on the 2.15-mm voxel discretization. Large individual variation remained. No linear-span or guitar percentile distribution follows. [Primary paper](https://www.eng.yale.edu/grablab/pubs/bullock_haptics2014.pdf)

**FR08 — Leitkam, Bush & Bix (2014), Determining Functional Finger Capabilities of Healthy Adults.** Primary abstract, DOI 10.1115/1.4026255. Weighted Fingertip Space distinguishes pad position, pad orientation and directional force capability. A theoretical hand used published 50th-percentile dimensions/ROM; motion capture from nine healthy individuals spanning approximately 2nd–95th hand-size percentiles tested the model. Experimental models occupied smaller average volumes but had higher functional weights within those volumes; modest dimensional/ROM changes affected those functional measures. The authors emphasize individual evaluation. Those participants' hand-size percentiles are not percentiles of functional capacity. Abstract-only access prevented extracting complete measurement protocols, sample demographics, full joint data or predictive errors. Treat as construct/method evidence, not population calibration. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24337062/)

**Review interpretation:** these studies make a constructive distinction useful for v2: “can contact a point,” “can orient a pad there,” “can exert force there,” and “can keep several contacts while moving” are different questions. The fixed-object task also imposes constraints absent from flat spreading. We should therefore resist replacing a simplistic reach number with a simplistic workspace number. None supplies a transferable multiplier from a piano span, anatomical length or free-space trajectory to guitar feasibility.

## Actual guitar execution evidence

**FR09 — Heijink & Meulenbroek (2002), On the Complexity of Classical Guitar Playing: Functional Adaptations to Task Constraints.** Primary author-hosted article, *Journal of Motor Behavior* 34(4):339–351. Six male professional classical guitarists aged 22–36 played 11-note scales with prescribed fingering. The experiment varied four/five-fret span, hand position and repositioning, measuring 3D finger motion, timing and self-rated complexity. Each of 12 conditions had 12 repetitions. Placement shifted within fret spaces when span demand increased; observed behavior supports treating a stopped fret as a contact region rather than a single mandated wire coordinate. This is genuine execution evidence for those sequential tasks. It is **not simultaneous sustained-chord evidence**, population maximal-span data, a trial of arbitrary six-contact voicings, or calibration of 95/180-mm boundaries. No candidate-level success probability is derivable. [Primary paper](https://www.socsci.ru.nl/meulenbroek/Publications/Heijink%20en%20Meulenbroek%202002.pdf)

Execution evidence thus exists in adjacent guitar tasks; it would be inaccurate to say that no humans have been studied playing guitar. The narrower outstanding gap is independently observed execution of the target simultaneous voicings, with compatible technique, instrument/setup, contact/damping semantics and subject measurements. This review found no applicable population-labelled chord-capacity dataset.

## Recommended interpretation for the v2 envelope

1. **Use two separately named layers of description.** Instrument demand may include longitudinal wire span, minimum contact-cell separation, lateral separation and topology. Reference measurements may include flat index–little or thumb–little spread. Keep both measurement definitions in the output instead of renaming their comparison “percentage of players who can play.”
2. **Prefer a table of observed reference quantiles to a synthetic percentile person.** FR01 supplies sample quartiles and dispersion. It does not provide individual matched all-pair spans, segment lengths, joint limits, pad sizes and strength. Combining marginal quantiles into one skeleton is not measured population sampling.
3. **Do not fit a normal tail to obtain a convenient boundary.** The reported sample medians/quartiles can be used directly. Estimated 5th/95th values from mean±1.645 SD would be model-derived approximations, not reported measurements; no such tail estimates are recommended here.
4. **A demand/reference ratio can be dimensional context only.** If a geometry feature is divided by a reference span, name numerator, denominator, landmark mismatch, side and sample. A ratio above one does not prove failure; below one does not prove execution or comfort. Prefer publishing the two quantities alongside the ratio so its limitations remain inspectable.
5. **Do not convert group count into anatomy.** Assignment ambiguity, bars/rolls, thumb support and simultaneous clearance remain separate from span distribution. A non-thumb 2–5 comparison does not address thumb-over cases, and a thumb–little flat spread does not solve thumb wrapping around a guitar neck.
6. **Keep uncertainty categorical rather than inventing covariance.** FR01's two-variable R² supports an association in those strata; it cannot provide a full multivariate hand distribution. FR07's variability and FR08's individualized modeling caution against assuming uniform scaling preserves functionality. There is no verified covariance matrix here for the complete proposed guitar hand model.
7. **Do not modify verdicts or ranking from this material.** These references can improve the interpretation of physical demand in a research artifact. They provide no new generic REJECT rule, no human PASS probability and no comfort penalty.

## Provenance and remaining gaps

The numeric span rows were transcribed from primary tables with centimetre-to-millimetre conversion only. FR02 uses source millimetres and preserves median/quartile semantics. PDF text extraction was available; screenshot attempts for FR01 failed with cache errors, so visual page verification is not claimed. Numeric values were cross-checked against the primary text/table locations, not a blog's redrawn graph. Primary abstract-only studies are explicitly marked. No raw individual data or unpublished values were inferred.

The useful next missing evidence is a protocol-matched fretting measurement: simultaneous assigned fingers under specified neck geometry, posture, force and clearance, with measured outcomes. A well-specified pair-contact task would still be a pair task; its measured distribution would not automatically generalize to four fingers plus thumb. Acquiring original all-pair musician data and repeatability information could improve reference description, but importing it must preserve landmark and posture definitions.

**Continuity with the frozen Physical review (session-verified conclusion, not a fresh artifact re-audit):** the explicit hand model adds conditional pose/clearance witnesses but omits constructive barres, thumb and strict damping; its model-only failures do not prove human impossibility. Its reference skeleton and one-axis sensitivity scenarios are not measured population profiles. This contribution neither reopens replacement nor proposes promoting that model. Its new conclusion is limited to using measured functional-span references with explicit construct boundaries.
